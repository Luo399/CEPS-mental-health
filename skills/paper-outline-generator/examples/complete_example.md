# Example: Climate Change Impact on Coastal Ecosystems - Comprehensive Outline

## Paper Information
- **Type**: Empirical Research Paper
- **Discipline**: Marine Biology/Environmental Science
- **Target Audience**: Journal of Marine Biology & Ecology
- **Estimated Length**: 8,000-10,000 words

## Overall Structure
Standard IMRAD structure with extended methodology and results sections, appropriate for ecological studies with field data collection and statistical analysis.

## Detailed Section Breakdown

### 1. Title Page
**Purpose**: Provide essential publication information
**Key Points**:
1. Clear, descriptive title reflecting study focus
2. Complete author affiliations and contact information
3. 4-6 relevant keywords for indexing
**Expected Length**: 1 page
**Common Pitfalls to Avoid**: Vague titles, incomplete author information, inappropriate keywords

### 2. Abstract (250-300 words)
**Purpose**: Concise summary of entire study
**Key Points**:
1. Background: Climate change threats to coastal ecosystems
2. Objectives: Assess specific impacts on [target species/communities]
3. Methods: Field sampling locations, measurement techniques, statistical analyses
4. Results: Key quantitative findings with statistical significance
5. Conclusions: Main implications for conservation and management
**Research Questions**:
- How has sea temperature rise affected species distribution?
- What is the relationship between ocean acidification and coral health?
- How do extreme weather events impact coastal biodiversity?
**Expected Length**: 250-300 words
**Common Pitfalls to Avoid**: Including results not discussed in paper, using jargon without explanation, omitting key findings

### 3. Introduction (1,200-1,500 words)
**Purpose**: Establish context, justify study, state objectives
**Key Points**:
1. Global context of climate change and coastal ecosystems
2. Specific vulnerabilities of study area/species
3. Review of previous research on similar topics
4. Identification of research gaps
5. Clear statement of research questions/hypotheses
6. Study objectives and significance
**Methodological Considerations**:
- Start broad (global climate change) → narrow (specific ecosystem)
- Cite key literature from last 5-10 years
- Clearly articulate contribution to field
**Expected Length**: 1,200-1,500 words
**Common Pitfalls to Avoid**: Overly long literature review, vague research questions, failing to establish significance

### 4. Methods (2,000-2,500 words)
**Purpose**: Detailed description for replication
**Key Points**:
1. Study area description (location, characteristics, justification)
2. Sampling design (transects, quadrats, temporal frequency)
3. Measurement techniques (equipment, protocols, calibration)
4. Environmental variables measured (temperature, pH, salinity, etc.)
5. Biological variables measured (species abundance, diversity indices, health indicators)
6. Statistical analyses (software, tests, significance levels)
7. Ethical considerations and permits
**Research Questions Alignment**:
- Methods should directly address each research question
- Include power analysis for sample size justification
- Detail quality control procedures
**Expected Length**: 2,000-2,500 words
**Common Pitfalls to Avoid**: Insufficient detail for replication, inappropriate statistical methods, missing ethical considerations

### 5. Results (1,500-2,000 words)
**Purpose**: Objective presentation of findings
**Key Points**:
1. Descriptive statistics for all variables
2. Results of hypothesis testing
3. Temporal trends and spatial patterns
4. Relationships between environmental and biological variables
5. Statistical significance levels
**Methodological Considerations**:
- Present in logical order matching research questions
- Use tables and figures effectively (not repeating information)
- Report exact p-values, confidence intervals
- Include effect sizes where appropriate
**Expected Length**: 1,500-2,000 words
**Common Pitfalls to Avoid**: Interpreting results (save for discussion), selective reporting, poor figure/table design

### 6. Discussion (2,000-2,500 words)
**Purpose**: Interpret results, compare with literature, discuss implications
**Key Points**:
1. Interpretation of key findings
2. Comparison with previous studies (agreements, disagreements)
3. Explanation of unexpected results
4. Theoretical implications for ecological theory
5. Practical implications for conservation/management
6. Study limitations and their impact
7. Future research directions
**Methodological Considerations**:
- Structure by research question or theme
- Acknowledge limitations honestly but not excessively
- Connect findings to broader ecological concepts
**Expected Length**: 2,000-2,500 words
**Common Pitfalls to Avoid**: Repeating results without interpretation, ignoring contradictory literature, overstating implications

### 7. Conclusion (500-800 words)
**Purpose**: Summarize key findings and contributions
**Key Points**:
1. Concise restatement of main findings
2. Answer to each research question
3. Contribution to field of marine ecology
4. Final recommendations for policy/practice
**Methodological Considerations**:
- Avoid introducing new information
- Be specific about contributions
- End with forward-looking statement
**Expected Length**: 500-800 words
**Common Pitfalls to Avoid**: Introducing new arguments, being too vague, repeating discussion points

### 8. References
**Purpose**: Acknowledge sources, enable verification
**Key Points**:
1. Consistent citation style (APA 7th recommended)
2. Include all cited works
3. Verify accuracy of all references
**Expected Length**: 2-3 pages
**Common Pitfalls to Avoid**: Missing citations, formatting errors, outdated references

### 9. Appendices (if needed)
**Purpose**: Supplementary material
**Key Points**:
1. Detailed methodological protocols
2. Additional data tables
3. Survey instruments
4. Ethical approval documents
**Expected Length**: Variable
**Common Pitfalls to Avoid**: Including essential information only in appendices, poor organization

## Writing Timeline & Milestones

### Phase 1: Preparation (Weeks 1-2)
- Complete literature review
- Finalize research questions
- Draft methodology section
- Create data analysis plan

### Phase 2: Writing Core Sections (Weeks 3-5)
- Write methods section (Week 3)
- Write results section (Week 4)
- Write introduction and discussion (Week 5)

### Phase 3: Refinement (Weeks 6-7)
- Write abstract and conclusion
- Create tables and figures
- Format references
- First complete draft

### Phase 4: Revision (Weeks 8-10)
- Self-revision (Week 8)
- Peer feedback incorporation (Week 9)
- Final polishing and submission (Week 10)
